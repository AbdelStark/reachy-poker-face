# Security and privacy

This is entertainment, not lie detection. Do not infer dishonesty, identity, health, or mental state from its output. Do not use it for screening or consequential decisions.

The explicit `?preview=1&fixture=1` route is an offline game-mechanics fixture, not Jev. Its numbers and pick are fixed by statement slot and independent of player text. It disables relay configuration, microphone and clip consent, ranking, and calibration-trace export; it does not play game lines. Do not combine its synthetic results with real-model evaluation data. Plain `?preview=1` has no built-in answers and may be connected to a real relay if the user configures one.

The app sends statement text and a small game state to the configured relay, which sends them to TypeSafe for a Jev judgment. The relay does not intentionally log request bodies or API keys. No statement text is saved to browser storage by this app. Numeric settings and, only if the player types a nickname, nickname scores are saved in browser local storage; the UI can clear those scores. The unnamed session score stays in the current tab. A browser speech-recognition provider may process device-microphone audio off-device when the user checks the separate per-round browser-microphone consent box and presses **Use browser microphone**; this is independent of the TypeSafe request. A final transcript auto-locks after a 1.5-second pause and then goes to the configured Jev relay. Revoking consent stops recognition and fences late callbacks, but cannot retract audio already processed by the provider or a Jev request already sent. Browser permission alone is not informed consent from everyone audible.

Resetting a round aborts the current browser-to-relay fetch and prevents late Jev or browser-recognition results from changing the next round. This is a local result fence, not remote deletion or cancellation: statement text already received by the relay or TypeSafe may still have been processed or billed. A reset also cannot undo audio or motion already started on a robot.

A failed live cue does not lock its statement and requests a neutral game pose if motion is armed; the request is not proof the robot moved or stopped. A failed final pick makes at most one additional Jev request before falling back to an explicitly random, unranked result. Both requests may be processed or billed even if the browser later resets or loses the reply. Do not use a retry or the neutral request as a physical safety guarantee.

The optional robot-audio path is off by default and needs separate consent from everyone audible for each round. The browser holds at most 15 seconds of robot-stream samples in tab memory, resamples to 16 kHz PCM, and sends it only to an explicitly configured numeric-loopback ASR companion with a separate bearer token. Unchecking consent or leaving discards capture and aborts a pending request; an already-delivered request cannot be retracted. The companion does not log or store PCM or transcripts and accepts only bounded, finite raw PCM, but the browser, OS, and local model runtime may retain transient copies. Only returned statement text and delivery buckets enter the Jev request. Do not expose the ASR companion beyond loopback; it lacks the protections required for a public service. No robot microphone or actual ASR model has been evaluated here.

Optional robot-speaker output is separately off by default. Only complete, fixed game lines are sent to the authenticated numeric-loopback TTS companion; player statements and free-form model text are not. A Jev-backed final line uses a fixed phrase for the validated cue and explicitly calls it a game guess, not proof. Fallback picks speak no model cue. The companion invokes host-installed eSpeak NG and FFmpeg without a shell and returns bounded PCM16 WAV. The browser validates the WAV before using the pinned SDK's audio upload/play API. Statement input and clip recording stay closed until the operator advances past the opening line; the operator must wait until it sounds finished, because an early click can still admit intro audio to the microphone or intro visuals to a clip. The transition cancels any still-active browser speech and requests cancellation of robot speech. A reset, microphone capture, mode change, or app exit also aborts pending TTS and requests cancellation of an uploaded clip, but the SDK does not acknowledge that robot playback has ended or that cancellation silenced the speaker. An upload in progress may finish after cancellation; the app then requests cancellation for its returned ID before invoking play. No live speaker, latency, cancellation, or voice-quality claim has been verified. Do not expose the companion beyond loopback or treat its token as a public-service credential.

Completed-round traces are kept in tab memory only (up to 100), with statement text excluded by default. Including text needs separate per-round consent before the round starts; the checkbox resets each round. Trace files never include nicknames, audio, or video. Once exported, files can be retained or shared outside the app, so inspect them before doing so. The calibration reader reports only aggregate numbers and model IDs; it does not print statement text or malformed duplicate key names. Its high-confidence accuracy is descriptive and can be unstable or empty; neither it nor the stored `correct` flag independently verifies a player's reveal.

Clip capture is off by default and requires a fresh consent checkbox for each round. It records canvas video only, without an audio track or statement text; recording stops after 30 seconds or when encoded chunks would exceed 16 MB. The host can stop and discard an in-progress recording without resetting the round, or discard a finished local clip before download or sharing. The native file-share action appears only when the browser reports file-share support and requires a separate click after the reveal; the app does not choose a destination or upload automatically. A cancelled share leaves the local clip available. The blob remains in browser memory until discard, a new round, or tab exit; downloading or sharing creates a separate copy. Do not check the box without consent from everyone visible, and use the discard control immediately if consent is withdrawn. Once a destination receives a clip, the app cannot revoke it; browser/OS internals may retain transient copies even after local discard. Share-sheet behavior is fake-browser tested, not verified on mobile hardware.

Keep the TypeSafe API key server-side. The development relay requires a strong bearer token, checks an exact browser origin, accepts only pinned live/final question wires and bounded game-shaped states, limits body size and concurrent requests, caps authenticated POST attempts at 30 per minute per process, and returns generic upstream errors. The bank pins must be reviewed together with any question wording or option change. Its rate window resets on process restart and does not measure billing or enforce a spending budget. These controls are not a full public-service deployment: add TLS, durable abuse limits, secret rotation, and cost monitoring before exposing a relay to the internet. An origin header is not authentication; the bearer token is required even when the origin matches. Do not share the token with untrusted clients.

Robot motion is expressive only and off until explicitly armed for the current tab. Mounting the connected app does not request a pose; arming requests neutral, after which game reactions may send bounded SDK targets. Disarming prevents new game pose requests and disables antenna-tap controls, but does not prove that queued motion stopped. If armed, leaving requests a neutral pose; that too is a request, not an acknowledged safe state. Its ranges, neutral pose, physical antenna-tap detection, and leave behavior require a real-device safety check before unattended use. Retain a physical stop and use it if a motion or actuator behaves unexpectedly.
